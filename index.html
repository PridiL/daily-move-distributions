Method. Daily change = close minus previous close on Barchart's back-adjusted (additive) nearest-futures series, so contract rolls are not counted as moves. % change divides by the previous day's unadjusted close. Points are the quote unit (e.g. $/oz for Gold, index points for the E-minis); currency futures are in pips of the futures quote, which is USD per foreign unit (inverse of USD/JPY, USD/CHF, USD/CAD, USD/MXN, USD/ZAR, USD/BRL). Days after a data hole of more than 7 calendar days are left out. Weekly. From the last trading day of one week (Monday to Sunday) to the last trading day of the next, normally Friday to Friday: the sum of that week's daily changes, so rolls and the dropped bad prints are handled exactly as in the daily view. Weekly % divides by the previous week's last unadjusted close. A week counts only when its days run unbroken from the previous week's close. Dates on weekly moves are the week's last trading day. High & low timing. Up or down = the week's (month's) close against the previous week's (month's) close, the same change as the weekly distribution. The highest day is the day whose high is the week's high, the lowest day the one whose low is the week's low, on Barchart's daily bars (back-adjusted for futures, so a roll inside the week cannot fake a high). Only full Monday–Friday weeks count, so a holiday cannot lower one weekday's odds. Weeks of the month are by day of the month: Week 1 = 1st–7th, Week 2 = 8th–14th, Week 3 = 15th–21st, Week 4 = 22nd to month end (more trading days). Ties split the count. The reversal of an up week (month) is the first day after its low that closes above the day before; of a down week (month), the first day after its high that closes below the day before. It is never on the extreme's own day, so Monday cannot be an up week's reversal day; None means no such close came before the period ended. The benchmark is a driftless random walk (24 steps a trading day, 200,000 simulated weeks or months for each layout of trading days per slot), conditioned on the same up or down close and averaged over the product's own weeks and months. z is a binomial z-score against it; with 90 products × 4 scenarios × 2 extremes × 4–5 slots = 3,240 cells, about 9 reach |z| ≥ 3 by luck alone. FX pairs (spot). Barchart daily spot closes, no contract rolls; pips are 0.01 for JPY pairs and 0.0001 for the rest. Spot trades on some exchange holidays, so these pairs have a few more days than the futures. Each cross was checked against its two USD legs (EUR/JPY against EUR/USD × USD/JPY); closes across all pairs that spiked more than 1% away from the legs and reverted the next day are treated as bad prints and left out, and each pair's page lists its dates. Reading it. Excess kurtosis above 0 means fatter tails and a taller middle than a normal curve: more quiet days and more extreme days. The log scale makes the tails visible. Points mix 26 years of very different price levels (Gold was $280 in 2000), so the % view is the fairer comparison across time; the yearly chart shows how far the spread moved. Bitcoin and Ether micros use the contract symbol Barchart resolves, which it does not back-adjust, so their roll days remain in.
