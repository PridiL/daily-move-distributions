Method. Daily change = close minus previous close on Barchart's back-adjusted (additive) nearest-futures series, so contract rolls are not counted as moves. % change divides by the previous day's unadjusted close. Points are the quote unit (e.g. $/oz for Gold, index points for the E-minis); currency futures are in pips of the futures quote, which is USD per foreign unit (inverse of USD/JPY, USD/CHF, USD/CAD, USD/MXN, USD/ZAR, USD/BRL). Days after a data hole of more than 7 calendar days are left out. FX pairs (spot). Barchart daily spot closes, no contract rolls; pips are 0.01 for JPY pairs and 0.0001 for the rest. Spot trades on some exchange holidays, so these pairs have a few more days than the futures. Each cross was checked against its two USD legs (EUR/JPY against EUR/USD × USD/JPY); closes across all pairs that spiked more than 1% away from the legs and reverted the next day are treated as bad prints and left out, and each pair's page lists its dates. Reading it. Excess kurtosis above 0 means fatter tails and a taller middle than a normal curve: more quiet days and more extreme days. The log scale makes the tails visible. Points mix 26 years of very different price levels (Gold was $280 in 2000), so the % view is the fairer comparison across time; the yearly chart shows how far the spread moved. Bitcoin and Ether micros use the contract symbol Barchart resolves, which it does not back-adjust, so their roll days remain in.
